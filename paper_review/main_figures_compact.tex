\begin{abstract}
Decentralized federated learning (DFL) removes the central aggregation server of conventional federated learning (CFL), thereby decreasing dependence on a single coordinating entity. Nevertheless, the performance of DFL may decline due to non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) data, Byzantine attacks, and noisy communication channels. To address data heterogeneity, we introduce a novel class-aware ring training (CART), which leverages lightweight per-class performance metrics to inform local training processes. We subsequently examine the individual and combined effects of Byzantine attacks and channel noise to assess their respective impacts on DFL performance. To counteract Byzantine attacks, we utilize a snapshot selection (SS) approach, while an expectation-based mitigation (EBM) approach is employed to manage channel noise. CART is assessed using IID and non-IID CIFAR-10 datasets under conditions that are clean, Byzantine-only, noise-only, and a combination of attack and noise. The findings indicate that CART maintains competitiveness across most scenarios and enhances accuracy and stability under severe non-IID conditions with concurrent Byzantine attacks and channel noise.
% Consumer edge systems increasingly rely on distributed learning across mobile devices, sensors, cameras, and Internet-of-Things environments. Federated learning keeps raw data local, but conventional designs still depend on a central aggregation server. Decentralized federated learning removes that server, reducing centralized coordination while increasing exposure to heterogeneous data, unreliable communication, and compromised participants. This paper proposes Class-Aware Ring Training (CART), a decentralized ring-training method that propagates model parameters together with a lightweight ClassRegistry containing verified per-class performance metadata. CART uses the registry to estimate class-specific learning gaps and adapt a proximal regularization term during local training. Snapshot Selection (SS) mitigates Byzantine model corruption, while Expectation-Based Mitigation (EBM) addresses channel noise. Enhanced EBM combines EBM with adaptive weight decay and should not be interpreted as EBM alone. We compare CART with a protocol-matched Merged baseline on IID and non-IID CIFAR-10 across clean, Byzantine, noisy-channel, and joint attack-plus-noise conditions. Across four seeds, CART and Merged are closely matched in most settings; in the most severe non-IID joint condition at high channel noise, CART yields a higher mean accuracy with lower across-seed variation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Federated learning (FL) facilitates collaborative model training without the necessity of sharing raw local data, with FedAvg serving as the foundational framework characterized by local optimization followed by centralized aggregation \cite{mcmahan_fedavg}. In contrast, decentralized federated learning (DFL) removes the need for a central coordinator by enabling direct model exchanges through peer-to-peer, gossip, or ring-based communication \cite{balancingcomm,fedisp,pfedgame}. This approach mitigates the risk of a single point of failure but introduces novel training challenges. Notably, non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) client data can lead to local models diverging towards their respective data distributions, thereby diminishing overall performance \cite{zhao_non_iid,fedprox,scaffold}.

In addition to data heterogeneity, DFL is susceptible to two primary sources of model degradation: Byzantine attacks and communication noise. Byzantine clients may intentionally transmit corrupted model states, necessitating the development of robust aggregation and Byzantine-tolerant methodologies \cite{Byzantine_Tolerant_GD,geometric_median,fall_of_empires,analyzing_fl_adversarial,basil}. Conversely, unreliable communication links can randomly distort otherwise valid model parameters, prompting the need for channel noise-robust strategies \cite{unreliablecomm,wireless_edge_fl,robustwireless, NoiseComm}. Given that Byzantine attacks and channel noise originate from fundamentally distinct mechanisms, it is essential to comprehend their individual and combined impacts, as well as the efficacy of their respective mitigation strategies, to ensure robust DFL.

This study introduces a novel method termed class-aware ring training (CART), which incorporates lightweight class-level memory into decentralized ring training to address performance degradation in non-IID data scenarios. CART operates by propagating a ClassRegistry alongside the model, which records the highest locally verifiable per-class accuracy, including source, round, and support metadata, without the need to share raw data or class-specific model parameters. Upon receiving the model, each client utilizes class-performance discrepancies to adjust a bounded proximal regularization coefficient, thereby minimizing detrimental deviations from the incoming consensus model. CART is specifically designed to counteract non-IID forgetting, while snapshot selection (SS) \cite{basil} and expectation-based mitigation (EBM) \cite{NoiseComm} independently address Byzantine model corruption and communication noise, respectively. Existing studies have largely considered these two impairments separately; to the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate both Byzantine attacks and noisy communication channels within a unified DFL framework. We evaluate the framework against a protocol-matched Merged baseline over four random seeds using IID and non-IID CIFAR-10 partitions under Byzantine attacks, model-parameter noise, and their combined presence. The main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We developed a class-aware DFL method, CART, with lightweight memory and proximal regularization to mitigate non-IID forgetting without central servers or shared datasets.
    \item We evaluated the robustness of CART under Byzantine attacks and noisy communication, using SS and EBM as their respective mitigation methods, across IID and non-IID settings.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{DFL under Data Heterogeneity}
Data heterogeneity remains a fundamental challenge in FL, as non-IID client data can cause local models to drift toward their local distributions, slowing convergence and degrading global performance \cite{zhao_non_iid}. Several approaches have been developed to mitigate this problem in centralized server-based FL. FedProx introduces proximal regularization to limit local model deviation from the global model \cite{fedprox}, while SCAFFOLD employs control variates to correct client drift \cite{scaffold}. However, these methods rely on centralized coordination, making their direct application to DFL less straightforward.

In DFL, clients exchange model information directly over peer-to-peer networks without relying on a central coordinator, using various communication topologies and protocols \cite{beltran_dfl_survey,balancingcomm,fedisp,pfedgame}. Under non-IID data, learning performance depends not only on local data distributions but also on how model information propagates across the decentralized network \cite{chung_dfl_non_iid}. Existing approaches address this challenge through performance-based neighbor selection, such as PENS \cite{pens}, and knowledge-transfer mechanisms based on logits or teacher--student signals \cite{kd_fl_guide}. Nevertheless, preserving knowledge across heterogeneous clients with limited global information remains a key challenge in DFL \cite{beltran_dfl_survey,chung_dfl_non_iid}.

% \subsection{Decentralized and Heterogeneous Federated Learning}
% Communication-efficient FL began with centralized aggregation methods such as FedAvg \cite{mcmahan_fedavg}. Local SGD reduces synchronization frequency by performing several local updates between communication events \cite{local_sgd}, while adaptive federated optimization improves convergence through server-side adaptive optimizers \cite{adaptive_fedopt}. These methods establish important optimization principles, but decentralized learning must operate without a single server-side state.

% DFL has been studied under ring, peer-to-peer, dynamic, and unreliable topologies. Prior work examines communication and computation tradeoffs \cite{balancingcomm}, incremental subgradient-proximal ring training \cite{fedisp}, game-theoretic neighbor interactions \cite{pfedgame}, and unreliable communication \cite{unreliablecomm}. Ring-all-reduce approaches provide another distributed structure for Byzantine-aware learning \cite{ring_all_reduce_byz}. Broader surveys emphasize that topology, heterogeneity, optimization, and security are tightly coupled in non-IID DFL \cite{dfl_non_iid_survey}.

% Non-IID data remain a central source of client drift. Zhao et al. document accuracy degradation under strongly skewed client distributions \cite{zhao_non_iid}. FedProx limits movement from a reference model through a proximal term \cite{fedprox}, while SCAFFOLD uses control variates to correct client drift \cite{scaffold}. PENS uses performance-based neighbor selection for decentralized non-IID learning \cite{pens}, and knowledge-distillation approaches transfer information through logits or teacher-student signals \cite{kd_fl_guide}. CART shares the goal of preserving knowledge under heterogeneity but neither changes the ring topology nor exchanges logits; instead, it uses class-level performance metadata to control a proximal penalty.

\subsection{DFL under Byzantine Attack and Noisy Communication}
Byzantine robustness has been extensively studied in distributed learning, where malicious or faulty clients may transmit arbitrary model updates that disrupt training \cite{Byzantine_Tolerant_GD}. Robust aggregation methods, including geometric-median-based approaches, reduce the influence of corrupted updates \cite{geometric_median}; however, carefully designed attacks can circumvent such defenses \cite{fall_of_empires,analyzing_fl_adversarial}. The problem becomes more challenging under non-IID data, as heterogeneous benign updates may resemble anomalous or malicious updates \cite{byzantine_robust_hetero}. GAS addresses this issue through gradient splitting to improve Byzantine robustness under heterogeneous data \cite{gas}. In DFL, where robustness decisions rely primarily on locally available information, BASIL employs ring-based SS over bounded neighbor memory \cite{basil}, BALANCE uses local model similarity to filter potentially malicious neighboring models \cite{balance}, and Bristle combines transfer learning with performance-based model integration under Byzantine and non-IID settings \cite{bristle}.

Communication noise is another important source of performance degradation in DFL, as model information exchanged among clients can be corrupted by imperfect wireless links. Early studies on FL over noisy channels analyze the effects of communication errors on convergence and develop robust learning and communication mechanisms to mitigate such impairments \cite{wireless_edge_fl,robustwireless}. In particular, expectation- and worst-case-based optimization has been proposed to improve robustness against stochastic transmission errors \cite{NoiseComm}. More recent studies explicitly consider imperfect communication in DFL. Li et al. show that communication errors can accumulate during decentralized aggregation and develop an adaptive aggregation strategy based on network topology and channel conditions \cite{li_imperfect_dfl}. DFL with global momentum (DFLGM) further addresses non-ideal decentralized networks by combining differential model exchange with global momentum to suppress accumulated channel noise under heterogeneous data distributions \cite{li_dflgm}. These studies demonstrate that communication errors directly affect model exchange and consensus in DFL, motivating communication-aware learning and mitigation mechanisms for reliable decentralized training.

% Classical Byzantine-tolerant learning seeks gradient or model aggregation rules that remain useful when some participants are arbitrary \cite{Byzantine_Tolerant_GD}. Robust estimators such as the geometric median provide one basis for reducing corrupted influence \cite{geometric_median}. Yet Byzantine-tolerant stochastic optimization can fail under carefully constructed attacks \cite{fall_of_empires}, and adversarial analyses of FL show that robustness must be evaluated empirically rather than assumed \cite{analyzing_fl_adversarial}. Heterogeneous data further complicate robust learning because honest updates can naturally differ \cite{byzantine_robust_hetero}.

% In decentralized systems, defenses must operate with limited local information. BASIL uses a ring and bounded memory to perform Snapshot Selection before training \cite{basil}. BALANCE studies Byzantine-robust decentralized FL without centralized aggregation \cite{balance}, while Bristle considers Byzantine and non-IID decentralized environments using transfer learning and performance-based model integration \cite{bristle}. CART instead keeps the complete model intact and adds class-aware memory that influences local regularization.

% Communication noise requires a different treatment. Wireless edge-learning research studies distributed optimization under constrained or noisy channels \cite{wireless_edge_fl,robustwireless}. Robust FL with noisy communication introduces expectation-based mitigation for stochastic perturbations of transmitted model values \cite{NoiseComm}. Accordingly, SS and EBM are complementary rather than competing defenses: SS addresses adversarial model selection, whereas EBM addresses stochastic channel distortion.

\section{Problem Formulation and Method}
Consider $N$ clients indexed as $i\in \{1, 2, \ldots, N\}$ arranged in a ring-structured network topology. Client $i$ owns private dataset $\mathcal{D}_i$ and model parameters $\theta_i^t$ at round $t$. The centralized empirical objective is used only as a reference:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\theta} F(\theta)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{|\mathcal{D}_i|}{|\mathcal{D}|}F_i(\theta),
\,
F_i(\theta)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}_i|}\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}_i}\ell(\theta;x,y).
\end{equation}
There is no central parameter server. Before local training, each client combines its current model with one selected received snapshot:
\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{cons},i}^t=\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_i^t+\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{\mathrm{sel},i}^t.
\label{eq:consensus}
\end{equation}
When SS is disabled, $\theta_{\mathrm{sel},i}^t$ is the immediate predecessor snapshot when available. When SS is enabled, the client selects among snapshots retained in bounded neighbor memory. Equation~\eqref{eq:consensus} is pairwise decentralized consensus; SS only determines which snapshot enters that consensus.

\subsection{Snapshot Selection and Channel Noise}
Each client retains at most one recent snapshot from each of five clockwise senders. With SS, candidates first pass a relative parameter-distance plausibility check. The threshold is $0.35$ without channel noise and $\sigma+0.35$ when noise is present. Among plausible candidates, the receiver selects the snapshot with the lowest loss on its first local training batch; if none pass, it uses the minimum-distance candidate.

Channel noise is applied independently to each directed transmission. Let $d$ denote the number of trainable scalar parameters and $\theta$ the transmitted vector. The coordinate standard deviation scales with the model norm:
\begin{equation}
s(\theta,\sigma)=\frac{\sigma\|\theta\|_2}{\sqrt d}, \qquad
\widetilde{\theta}=\theta+\xi,\quad
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,s(\theta,\sigma)^2I).
\label{eq:noise}
\end{equation}
Thus, $\sigma$ controls relative model-parameter noise rather than a fixed coordinate variance.

The hidden attack is inactive before round 20. After activation, each attacked layer $\ell$ is transformed as
\begin{equation}
\psi_\ell=0.45\theta_\ell+0.55(-1.4\theta_\ell+\epsilon_\ell),
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_\ell$ is a zero-mean Gaussian perturbation with standard deviation $0.05\max(\operatorname{std}(\theta_\ell),0.01)$. When channel noise is also enabled, the attacked outbound model is subsequently perturbed independently for each receiver according to (\ref{eq:noise}). This ordering separates malicious model construction from stochastic link corruption.

\subsection{ClassRegistry and Class-Aware Proximal Control}
CART augments each transmitted snapshot with a ClassRegistry. For class $c$, it stores
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}[c]=(a_c,s_c,r_c,n_c),
\end{equation}
where $a_c$ is the best verified class accuracy, $s_c$ the source node, $r_c$ the round, and $n_c$ the support count. A received claim is considered only when both the transmitted record and the receiver's local class probe have positive support. If the received model obtains class accuracy $a_{\mathrm{recv},c}$ on that probe, improvement $a_{\mathrm{claim},c}$ is accepted when
\begin{equation}
a_{\mathrm{recv},c}\ge a_{\mathrm{claim},c}-0.05.
\end{equation}
This is an empirical consistency check rather than a cryptographic guarantee.

For each reliable class, CART compares the current model with the consensus reference. Let
\begin{equation}
a_{\mathrm{target},c}=\min(a_{\mathrm{registry},c},a_{\mathrm{ref},c}),
\end{equation}
where $a_{\mathrm{ref},c}$ is the class accuracy of the pairwise-consensus model. The supported class gap is
\begin{equation}
g_i^t=\operatorname{mean}_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{rel}}}
\max(0,a_{\mathrm{target},c}-a_{\mathrm{cur},c}).
\end{equation}
The gap is smoothed with $\beta=0.85$,
\begin{equation}
\bar g_i^t=0.85\bar g_i^{t-1}+0.15g_i^t,
\end{equation}
and converted to a bounded proximal coefficient
\begin{equation}
\mu_i^t=\operatorname{clip}(\gamma\bar g_i^t,0,\gamma).
\label{eq:mu}
\end{equation}
Local CART training minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_i(\theta)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_i|}\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{B}_i}\ell(\theta;x,y)
+\frac{\mu_i^t}{2}\|\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{cons},i}^t\|_2^2.
\label{eq:cartloss}
\end{equation}
The Merged baseline uses the same ring, consensus, SS, noise, optimizer, and EBM machinery but does not carry a ClassRegistry or apply the class-gap-driven proximal term.

\subsection{EBM and Enhanced EBM}
For noisy-channel runs, EBM augments the ordinary batch gradient $g=\nabla_\theta F_{\mathcal{B}}(\theta)$ with a second-order gradient-norm regularizer:
\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{EBM}}=g+\lambda_t\nabla_\theta\|g\|_2^2.
\label{eq:ebm}
\end{equation}
The resulting gradient is globally clipped to norm 5 before the CART proximal contribution is applied. Enhanced EBM means that EBM and adaptive weight decay are active together during local optimization; it is not a replacement for EBM. Results labeled Enhanced EBM therefore reflect the combined EBM plus adaptive weight-decay configuration. Under joint attack and noise, SS+Enhanced EBM adds Snapshot Selection to that same configuration.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{CART update at client $i$}
\label{alg:cart}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Current model, neighbor memory, local data, ClassRegistry
\STATE Select predecessor snapshot or apply SS when enabled
\STATE Form pairwise consensus using (\ref{eq:consensus})
\STATE Verify and merge supported registry claims
\STATE Evaluate current and consensus models on the local class probe
\STATE Compute $g_i^t$, update $\bar g_i^t$, and set $\mu_i^t$ using (\ref{eq:mu})
\STATE Train locally from $\theta_{\mathrm{cons},i}^t$ using (\ref{eq:cartloss})
\IF{EBM is enabled}
\STATE Apply the second-order term in (\ref{eq:ebm}) and gradient clipping
\ENDIF
\STATE Update supported registry entries from the local probe
\STATE Apply hidden attack to Byzantine outbound states when active
\STATE Apply independent channel noise when configured
\STATE Transmit model and registry to the next five clockwise clients
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}
Experiments use CIFAR-10 with 10 logical clients. Images are normalized to $[0,1]$, and training uses horizontal flips plus four-pixel padded random crops. The IID split distributes examples uniformly, while the non-IID split uses class-wise Dirichlet partitioning with $\alpha=0.2$. All clients use the same VGG-style TensorFlow model summarized in Table~\ref{tab:model_architecture}.

\subsection{Sigma-Oriented Robustness Comparison}

At $\sigma=0.2$, the IID joint conditions remain close to the attack-only reference. Merged changes from 0.820 under Hidden+SS to 0.812 with Hidden+Noise+SS and 0.813 with SS+Enhanced EBM. CART changes from 0.820 to 0.814 and 0.813, respectively. The low-noise setting therefore adds only a small penalty once Snapshot Selection is active.

The same pattern is visible under non-IID data, but at a lower absolute accuracy because the clients receive strongly heterogeneous class distributions. Merged records 0.438 under Hidden+SS, 0.424 under Hidden+Noise+SS, and 0.427 under the combined defense. CART records 0.441, 0.432, and 0.426. These values remain relatively close to the non-IID clean references of 0.477 and 0.473, although the across-seed variation is much larger than in the IID experiments.

At $\sigma=0.4$, channel noise creates a clearer separation between SS alone and the combined defense. For IID Merged, SS under joint attack and noise reaches 0.721, while SS+Enhanced EBM reaches 0.766. CART shows the same pattern, increasing from 0.718 to 0.765. Under non-IID data, Merged increases from 0.280 to 0.345 and CART from 0.285 to 0.344 when Enhanced EBM is added to SS. This does not imply that EBM alone is a Byzantine defense. Rather, SS continues to provide the Byzantine-oriented selection mechanism, while the EBM plus adaptive weight-decay configuration addresses the additional channel-noise stress.

The $\sigma=0.6$ setting is the strongest stress test. SS alone collapses to approximately 0.10 for both methods under both data splits. When SS is combined with Enhanced EBM, IID accuracy recovers to 0.635 for Merged and 0.628 for CART. The non-IID setting remains more difficult, reaching 0.184 for Merged and 0.223 for CART. The latter difference is descriptive rather than conclusive because only four seeds are available and Merged has substantially higher variability in this condition.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

The expanded results support several restrained conclusions. First, Merged and CART are effectively matched in the IID reference setting. Their clean and attack-only values are nearly identical, showing that the additional ClassRegistry and class-gap-driven proximal term do not create a large penalty when client data are homogeneous.

Second, non-IID data are the dominant source of the absolute accuracy reduction. Clean accuracy falls from about 0.84 under IID data to about 0.47 under the Dirichlet non-IID split. That lower ceiling should not be read as a failure of CART or Merged by itself. The non-IID experiment combines strong class heterogeneity with decentralized clockwise propagation and no central global aggregation step. Local training can therefore emphasize classes that are overrepresented at one client while information about other classes propagates only through the ring. The current study does not contain a topology ablation, so it cannot determine how much of the reduction comes from the non-IID partition and how much comes from the directed ring.

Third, robustness should be judged against the appropriate data-distribution baseline. Under non-IID data, Hidden+SS reaches 0.438 for Merged and 0.441 for CART compared with clean values of 0.477 and 0.473. At $\sigma=0.2$, the joint SS conditions remain in the same general range. This shows that the methods retain much of their non-IID clean performance under mild noise and attack even though they do not approach the much easier IID ceiling.

Fourth, the joint experiments demonstrate why SS and Enhanced EBM should not be conflated. SS handles adversarial snapshot selection but becomes fragile at high channel noise. Channel-oriented regularization without SS cannot protect against the hidden Byzantine attack. Combining SS with Enhanced EBM is therefore most useful when both failure modes occur simultaneously. At $\sigma=0.4$, the combined defense materially improves over SS alone for both approaches and both data splits. At $\sigma=0.6$, it prevents the complete collapse seen with SS alone, although non-IID performance remains difficult.

Fifth, the ablation results qualify the interpretation of Enhanced EBM. The plots show that adaptive weight decay is a substantial contributor to high-noise stability. Plain EBM does not consistently prevent collapse at the most severe noise level, and SS+WD can be comparable to SS+EBM+WD in some joint conditions. Accordingly, this paper reports Enhanced EBM as a combined configuration and does not attribute its behavior to EBM alone.

CART's strongest relative result in the principal table occurs at non-IID $\sigma=0.6$ with SS+Enhanced EBM, where its mean is 0.223 compared with 0.184 for Merged and its across-seed variability is smaller. This result is promising but remains descriptive. With four seeds, one dataset, and one non-IID concentration, the evidence is not sufficient for a general claim that CART is superior to Merged. A larger seed count, multiple Dirichlet concentrations, alternative decentralized topologies, and explicit ClassRegistry and proximal-control ablations would provide a stronger causal test.

The study also uses one 10-client topology, fixed five-snapshot memory and fanout, and one hidden attack applied to four clients from round 20. The channel model uses relative Gaussian parameter noise rather than packet loss, fading, delay, compression, or quantization. Registry verification is empirical and relies on each receiver's local class probe; it provides neither cryptographic integrity nor a formal Byzantine guarantee. Finally, the ClassRegistry reveals class-performance, source, round, and support metadata. Although it does not reveal raw examples, formal privacy analysis of this metadata remains future work.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper introduced CART, a class-aware extension to decentralized ring-based federated learning. CART combines pairwise decentralized consensus with a lightweight ClassRegistry and a bounded class-gap-driven proximal term. SS remains responsible for Byzantine-oriented snapshot selection, while EBM addresses noisy communication. Enhanced EBM means that EBM and adaptive weight decay are used together in the same noise-mitigation configuration.

The expanded evaluation shows that IID Merged and CART remain closely matched near the clean reference, while non-IID performance is lower because the learning problem is substantially more heterogeneous. Within that harder regime, both methods remain relatively close to their own non-IID clean baselines under the attack-only condition and mild joint noise. As channel noise increases, SS alone becomes insufficient, while the combination of SS and Enhanced EBM preserves substantially more accuracy. CART shows its largest relative advantage over Merged in the most severe non-IID joint condition, but broader experiments are required before generalizing that observation.
